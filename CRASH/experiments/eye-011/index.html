What Crash receives

Each trial is ten 32×32 RGB frames compressed into a ten-frame additive memory. A colored patch persists while random single-frame flashes come and go. Crash receives only three global channel sums from that memory, normalized by its total brightness. A second learner receives only the grayscale sum. Both predict an arbitrary binary outcome, A or B, and update their weights from correctness feedback.

Neither learner receives a color name, patch location, or the hidden category. The experiment supplies RGB channels, the ten-frame accumulation, channel-sum features, a binary choice task, and the learning rule. It tests learned color discrimination, not independent invention of colors, object recognition, or semantic understanding.

The two hidden color families are deliberately separated by dominant channels and are paired with A/B outcomes. Training uses one fixed palette and upper-half positions. Evaluation can use new shades and/or lower-half positions. Grayscale brightness is deliberately matched for the two families in the training palette, making it an important control.
