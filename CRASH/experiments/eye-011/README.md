# Eye 011: Uga Bunga Skittles

Isolated supervised color-association experiment. Each trial: ten full 32×32 RGB frames, a persistent 4×4 colored patch at a randomized location, and optional single-frame colored noise. The 10% additive accumulator compresses the frames. The learner gets **only normalized total RGB channel intensities** (plus a bias), not the raw image or object coordinates. A grayscale control gets only mean luminance and bias. Both are online logistic classifiers receiving binary A/B feedback. Their initial weights are zero; no color words or mappings are in either learner.

The environment, however, defines the two target families and their A/B association. This is supervised learning, not spontaneous discovery of categories. Training positions are upper rows and palette is fixed. Evaluation supports held-out lower positions and novel brightness/shades. Color family 0 is red-dominant, family 1 green-dominant. Their approximate luminance is matched in training, but noise and novel shades may introduce luminance differences. Both use the same samples, and evaluation does not update weights. A positive result establishes discriminative use of RGB channel summaries, **not** learning color names, spatial attention, or object concepts. No previous experiments modified.
