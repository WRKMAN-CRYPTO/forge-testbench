What is supplied?

Every scene has equal numbers of persistent red and green objects, with equal brightness within each color family. One small white marker appears directly above one colored object. The hidden A/B outcome depends on the marked object's color, never on the overall red/green totals.

The learner receives the accumulated RGB pixels, converted by a fixed front end into 4×4 candidate regions. It gets local color contrast and the brightness of the tile directly above each candidate. A trainable attention weight decides how much that neighboring cue matters. A trainable classifier maps the attended color signal to A/B. No target location or marker meaning is given to either learner.

The region grid, candidate detector, above-neighbor feature, and feedback are engineered. Success tests learned cue-to-color association within that architecture, not autonomous discovery of geometry or objects. The global control sees only scene-wide RGB totals. No learner updates during evaluation.
