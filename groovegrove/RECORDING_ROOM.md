# GrooveGrove: Three-Room Studio

## Purpose
Instrument Room → Practice Studio → Recording Room.
GrooveGrove is a musical autonomy experiment, not a quality-scoring game.

### Room 1: Instrument Room
- One physical drum kit, guitar, and violin live here.
- Taking, returning, or swapping physical instruments requires a visit.
- Voices need no equipment; existing voice identities stay intact.

### Room 2: Practice Studio
- The original studio coordinates, live practice and musical exchanges remain.
- Two musicians may practice nearby without forming a jam.
- Walls and doors reduce how far sound carries between rooms.

### Room 3: Recording Room
- Musicians may voluntarily visit and listen to a saved performance.
- The deck plays the actual scheduled synth events of a completed formal jam.
- Their recording exposure is counted without advancing musical practice or granting skills.
- The mobile canvas offers previous / next / play / stop.

## Authenticity and retention
- Historical jam counts do not contain full audio or note streams. Never fabricate tapes for past jams.
- Only newly completed formal jams are archived, with precise events, note origins, instrument timbres, and timing.
- Each clip records its ID, bar range, participants, note-event timing, pitch, duration, dynamics and expression.
- First pass retains 32 recent completed jams, each capped at 1200 actual scheduled events.
- Interrupted or silent jams never become fake recordings.
- Replaying a recording changes no motor skill, notes-practiced counters, learned books or Murmur vocal journal.

## Future scaling
Longer-term storage should move complete tapes out of the frequently transmitted world snapshot.
Introduce dedicated on-demand clip retrieval before increasing the retention limit.
