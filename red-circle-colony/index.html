Scouts discover contrast, grow and connect complete colonies, then fill each stable mature colony with individually stored circles. Choose classic packing, bubble births, or fluid settlement: identical mobile bubbles redistribute inside each discovered territory. No OCR, alphabet knowledge, or fixed-grid compression.

The background contrast mask is estimated from image borders, not from letter shapes. The agents have no idea what an image represents. Mature means a connected colony reaches the chosen pixel count and stops growing; it does not imply recognition. Circles stay entirely inside their colony mask, and no two circles overlap. Bubble births grow individually to the largest radius that fits, then pop if blocked. Fluid mode uses equal-radius mobile particles with repulsion, local empty-space pressure, damping, and periodic births after settling. Coverage is rasterized occupancy, not continuous area.

The heatmap counts how many of the independently discovered and packed colonies occupy each location. It is a diagnostic display, not compressed storage or a learned template. Circle-outline stacking reveals discovery and placement variation; the heatmap reveals coverage agreement. Adjustable consensus is a visualization of raw observations, not a stored template. A 95% packing target means some disagreement is expected.

Experimental boundaries

Packing is raster-based and greedy rather than mathematically optimal. 95% is measured as occupied foreground pixels divided by colony foreground pixels, not circle area divided by bounding-box area. A thin one-pixel stroke may only fit tiny circles. The comparison score measures normalized circle-mask overlap, not semantic identity. Export includes every circle's original x, y, radius, and colony identifier, plus source mask pixels for reproducibility.
