EXPERIMENT 005

Shape Ward

Shape language as gameplay role
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Dominant geometry can carry emotional and functional meaning before detail: rounded masses tend to feel soft or friendly, square masses stable and forceful, and triangular masses sharp, fast, or aggressive. The silhouette should communicate the role at thumbnail scale.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four original 16×16 sprites: a round welcoming critter, a square pushing brute, a triangular hunter, and a diamond-like guardian. The response game pairs each silhouette family with behavior instead of asking for another direct identification quiz. An always-visible neutral specimen strip makes render failure obvious immediately.

SELF-CRITIQUE

The round creature reads soft fastest, but its tiny feet weaken the circular mass. The square brute feels heavy, yet borders on being a literal box rather than a living creature. The triangular hunter communicates danger immediately, but its perfect symmetry makes it icon-like. The next shape-language pass should keep the dominant geometry while adding asymmetry and anatomy that feel alive.

EXPERIMENT 004

Pose Relay

Skeleton-first action poses
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Establish gesture with a simple skeleton before adding pixel mass. Leaning, reaching, twisting, and counterbalance can communicate action more clearly than extra detail.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four 16×16 workers built around different pose skeletons: lifter, thrower, catcher, and carrier.

SELF-CRITIQUE

Catcher reads strongest. Lifter and thrower improved, but torso masses still converge after color is added.

EXPERIMENT 003

Edge Runner

Outline contrast across backgrounds
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Outline color is contextual. A tinted outline can integrate a sprite better than pure black, but it still needs contrast against the actual background.

WHAT I PRACTICED

A short runner with five original 16×16 assets and changing background values.

SELF-CRITIQUE

Tinted outlines feel less detached, but selective outlining remained too conservative and some cave edges lose separation.

EXPERIMENT 002

Cluster Critters

Clusters, not confetti
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Build tiny sprites from deliberate connected color clusters. Isolated pixels often read as noise.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four original 16×16 creatures with different body geometry and restrained palettes.

SELF-CRITIQUE

The crab has the strongest broad silhouette. Some internal regions still fill space rather than describe volume.

EXPERIMENT 001

Silhouette Hunt

Silhouette separation
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Block the silhouette first. At 16×16, exaggerate pose and class-defining props; use asymmetry and empty space.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four original classes built around external shape: knight, mage, archer, and brute.

SELF-CRITIQUE

Archer reads strongest. Biggest weakness: all four bodies share too much central humanoid mass.
