EXPERIMENT 002

Cluster Critters

Clusters, not confetti
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Build tiny sprites from deliberate connected color clusters. Isolated pixels often read as noise; large clusters should describe planes, masses, and edges while preserving the silhouette at actual game scale.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four original 16×16 creatures with different body geometry: horned beetle, long-eared hopper, wide crab, and tall ghost. Each uses a restrained four-color palette and avoids decorative single-pixel speckle.

SELF-CRITIQUE

The crab has the strongest broad silhouette and the hopper’s ears separate it cleanly. The beetle is compact but its legs still feel diagrammatic. The ghost is readable yet too conventional, so the design lesson is weaker there. Cluster discipline improved, but several internal regions are still blocky rather than describing convincing volume. Next pass should make clusters follow form, not merely fill it.

EXPERIMENT 001

Silhouette Hunt

Silhouette separation
PLAY →
LESSON STUDIED

Block the silhouette first. At 16×16, exaggerate pose and class-defining props; use asymmetry and empty space so identity survives before color and internal detail.

WHAT I PRACTICED

Four original classes built around external shape: shield-and-sword knight, tall-hat mage, archer with bow negative space, and broad brute with club.

SELF-CRITIQUE

Archer reads strongest. Biggest weakness: all four bodies share too much central humanoid mass. Future silhouette work should alter stance and torso geometry more aggressively.

SOURCE
