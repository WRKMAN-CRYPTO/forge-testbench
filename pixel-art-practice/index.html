EXPERIMENT 008

Crew Call

Proportion hierarchy at 16×16
PLAY →
PRINCIPLE STUDIED

Realistic human ratios collapse at 16×16. Enlarge the head and design the torso/leg masses as deliberate blocks so the figure reads before details.

WHAT I ATTEMPTED

Four original workers: Scout, Hauler, Guard, and Miner. Each uses compressed 2–3-head proportions but changes mass hierarchy without handheld props rescuing the anatomy. An INK TEST exposes silhouette-only reads.

SOURCE

SpriteGen: How to Draw a Pixel Art Character (16×16 and 32×32)

SELF-CRITIQUE

Hauler is the clearest proportion study: broad torso, compact head/legs, no extra-limb noise. Miner also reads cleanly, but the helmet is doing some identity work. Scout is recognizably human yet too skeletal below the torso. Guard is readable but the shoulder width and side arm blocks still risk becoming protrusion noise.

BENCHMARK

Estimated 33/53. No head-count ambiguity. Main hard-fail risk remains limb-count ambiguity on narrow figures. Rival pressure: usable, not competitive yet.
